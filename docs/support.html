Readings

Where does the reading come from?

From the pressure sensor built into your iPhone (every iPhone since iPhone 6 has one). Nothing comes from the internet or a weather service, so it works in airplane mode and out of signal. The app shows what the sensor reads.

How often is a reading stored?

Every five minutes while the app is open. While it is closed, iOS lets the app check in the background from time to time, often a few times a day, and each check stores one reading. iOS decides when, based on how you use your phone; it does not run these checks after you swipe the app away in the app switcher, in Low Power Mode, or with Background App Refresh off for Barometer (Settings, General, Background App Refresh).

Why are there gaps in the chart?

A gap is a time with no stored reading, usually while the app was closed and iOS had not let it check. The chart leaves gaps empty and marks them; it never draws a line across them. A single background reading shows as a dot.

What do Rising, Steady and Falling mean?

The change from the reading about three hours earlier. Less than 1 hPa either way is Steady. The arrow and the words, like "Falling, 3.1 hPa in 3 hours", say exactly what changed. With no reading from one to three and a half hours ago there is no trend yet.

The reading changed when I went upstairs. Why?

Air pressure drops as you go up, about 1 hPa for every 8 meters (27 feet). Your phone feels the difference between floors. That is how the altimeter works, and why a big change from a lift or a plane never sets off an alert.

Sea level and the altimeter

What is sea level pressure?

The reading adjusted to what it would be at sea level, so it can be compared with other places. The app needs your elevation for it: from your location once on the Altimeter tab, or typed in Settings (Pro). The gauge says which one is in use.

How does the altimeter work without signal?

It follows the change in pressure. Tap Reset here at the start of a climb, and it shows how far you have gone up or down since. Weather moves pressure too, so for the best reading reset just before you set off.

Why does it ask for my location?

Only on the Altimeter tab, only when you tap Use my location, and only while the app is open, to get a starting elevation once. Your location stays on your iPhone. Everything works with location off.

Alerts, notes and export

How does the change alert work?

Turn it on in History (the bell) or Settings, and choose an amount from 2 to 6 hPa. When the stored readings show a rise or a fall of that much within three hours, you get a notification such as "Pressure fell 4 hPa in the last 3 hours." One change alerts once. It is checked each time a reading is stored, so while the app is closed it depends on how often iOS lets the app check, and it can be late.

How do I add a note?

Tap the tag on the gauge, or Add under Notes in History. Pick Headache, Joint pain, Fishing, Hike, or type your own label. The note sits on the chart with the pressure and trend at that moment. Hold a note in the list to delete it.

What is in the PDF and the CSV?

The PDF is one page: the chart for 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days, the high, low and change, and your notes with the pressure at each. The CSV has every stored reading and note with the time and the pressure.

Pro

What is free and what is Pro?

Free: the live barometer, the altimeter, every unit, sea level from your location, the last 24 hours of history and one note. Pro adds the 7 and 30 day charts, the change alert, notes on the chart, PDF and CSV export, a typed elevation for sea level, and the Marine and Plain dials. Prices are shown in the app from the App Store.

How do I restore a purchase?

Settings, Restore purchases, or Restore purchases on any Pro screen, signed in with the same Apple ID.

How do I cancel?

In your iPhone's Settings, your name, Subscriptions, Barometer. Your readings and notes stay on your iPhone.
